Dutch Reformed Church

Welcome to Aberdeen's Dutch Reformed Church, a landmark at the heart of the town's story. In 1855, a new congregation was established for farming families living far from Graaff-Reinet. The settlement grew on the farm Brakkefontein, acquired from Jan Vorster, and was named Aberdeen after the Scottish birthplace of Reverend Andrew Murray Senior. The church developed in stages: an earlier building was completed in the eighteen sixties, and the striking tower was added in the early twentieth century. As you look up, imagine families gathering here after long journeys across the Karoo. The church was more than a place of worship. For the early town, it was an anchor for community life, education and new streets. Our next stop takes you from spiritual life to civic life.
