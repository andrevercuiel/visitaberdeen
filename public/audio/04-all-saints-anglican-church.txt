All Saints Anglican Church

At All Saints Anglican Church, choose one window you can see comfortably from the public approach. Trace it with your eye, starting at the bottom of one side. Go up the straight edge, around the curve and on to the point. Now follow the other side back down. That meeting of two curves is one of the easiest ways to recognise the Gothic Revival character of this little church. Once you have seen it in a single opening, step back until you can take in the length of wall around it.

The regional heritage guide dates All Saints to eighteen eighty-four. Think of where that places it in the walk. The Dutch Reformed congregation had been established for nearly thirty years. The Methodist community had recently built its stone church. By the middle of the eighteen eighties, an Anglican congregation too had made a lasting place for itself in the streets of Aberdeen. A visitor who only saw the great clock tower might never guess how many different places of worship belonged to this small town.

Gothic Revival can sound like a grand term for a quiet building. Here is a way to make sense of it. Nineteenth-century builders returned to forms familiar from much older churches, especially pointed arches. The point gives even a narrow opening an emphatic upward direction. Look at the stone on either side of the window and then at the empty space framed by it. One gives the wall its weight. The other invites your eye through the wall and into the building. From outside, the glass may look dark. A person sitting inside on the same morning receives the daylight coming the other way.

Consider what that means during a service. Someone arriving through the door turns towards the place from which the prayers or readings are led. Windows along a side wall can bring light to the congregation without everyone having to face directly into the sun. Even from the street you can ask which openings would receive morning light, and which might stay in shade. The answers will depend on where you stand and when you visit. Give the building a few seconds of your own attention before walking on.

There is another detail worth carrying away, and it belongs to the name. All Saints is a dedication with room for people whose names may never appear on a plaque. Baptisms, marriages, funerals and ordinary Sundays can give one building a place in many family histories. If the church is closed, you can appreciate much of its design from outside. Take one last look at the pointed window you chose at the beginning. At our next stop, a private house uses light and coloured glass for an altogether different purpose.
