Jan Vorster and Brakkefontein

Our final stop returns us to the beginning of the town, but not the beginning of human history here. Long before Aberdeen was established, indigenous communities lived and travelled through the wider Camdeboo landscape. European farming expanded into the region in the eighteenth century, bringing profound change. In 1855, land on the farm Brakkefontein, owned by Jan Vorster, was acquired for a new Dutch Reformed congregation. The town developed around that decision, and plots were sold to residents in the years that followed. It took its name from Aberdeen in Scotland, the birthplace of Reverend Andrew Murray Senior. As your Discovery Route ends, remember that a town's story is much larger than its formal founding date. Keep exploring its people, cultures, landscapes and living traditions. Thank you for discovering Aberdeen.
